## Main findings

- All one-user fixtures reported zero slice reconfigurations. These fixtures configure only one slice, so dynamic reallocation between slices is not exercised.
- Among the ten-user fixtures, `u10-policy-complete-vm` had the shortest wall time (47.37 seconds). `u10-scope-transport` had the longest wall time (146.55 seconds) and highest peak resident memory (2.85 GiB).
- `u10-migration-off` produced the most slice reconfigurations: 31,330, corresponding to 62,660 seconds of aggregate slice outage with the default 2-second delay.
- The dynamic weighted three-slice scenario produced 21,394 reconfigurations at a 2-second delay, but only 782 at a 60-second delay. Despite the 96.35% reduction in reconfiguration count, aggregate outage increased by 9.66%, from 42,788 to 46,920 seconds.
- Transport-only dynamic slicing produced only 28 reconfigurations, whereas wireless-only slicing produced 21,422. For this workload, almost all observed reallocation activity came from the wireless part of the slice.
- Static slicing produced more events and tuples and had far lower application-loop latency than the comparable dynamic configuration, although the benchmark itself took longer and used more memory. Wall time is therefore not a proxy for simulated service quality.
- The ten-user baseline completed 8 VM migrations and 70 handoffs, with 56.963 seconds of mean migration downtime and 0.170537% tuple loss. The edge-only destination fixture completed the most migrations (19), followed by the container-migration fixture (18).
- Complete-VM and container migration produced the highest ten-user tuple-loss rates: 62.817776% and 55.136525%, respectively. Other ten-user fixtures remained below 0.64% loss.
- Among one-user runs, complete-VM and container migration each lost about 39% of tuple observations. All other one-user fixtures stayed below 0.077% loss.

## Metric interpretation

- `slice_reconfigurations` counts completed dynamic slice reallocation processes reported during the simulation.
- `slice_outage_seconds` is defined as `slice_reconfigurations × reallocation_delay_seconds`. It is an aggregate penalty and may exceed the simulated duration because it is summed across reconfiguration occurrences; it is not the duration of one global system outage.
- `slice_received_bandwidth_bits_per_second_sum` adds the bandwidth allocations received by each slice during reconfiguration. Its numerical unit remains bit/s because it is a sum of sampled allocation values, not bandwidth integrated over time.
- Benchmark wall time and peak RSS describe the cost of running the simulator. Event, tuple, loop, migration, outage, and allocation measurements describe simulated behavior and should be interpreted separately.
